# Infant RSV and childhood flu policy: PK, GB, US, CA, AE, SA

Researched 27 Oct 2026 for Palna's optional "ask your doctor" reminders. All sources were accessed on 27 Oct 2026, and page dates are given where the page shows one. A pediatrician still has to review this.

Abbreviations:
- **mAb**: the infant RSV antibody injection. Nirsevimab is sold as Beyfortus; clesrovimab as Enflonsia.
- **RSVpreF**: the maternal RSV vaccine (Abrysvo), given in pregnancy.
- **LAIV**: the nasal-spray flu vaccine.
- **IIV**: an injected flu vaccine.

---

## Pakistan (PK)

### RSV
1. **Not offered.** There is no infant RSV antibody and no maternal RSV vaccine in the national Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI). The Federal Directorate of Immunization says the programme protects children aged 0–15 months against 12 diseases: TB, polio, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, Hib/meningitis, pneumonia, diarrhoea (rotavirus), measles, rubella and typhoid. HPV is offered for girls. RSV is not on the list.
   - Source: https://www.epi.gov.pk/immunization-schedule/ (the schedule is an image uploaded in Oct 2023; the site lists the diseases covered).
   - A Feb 2025 commentary in the Journal of the Pakistan Medical Association only argues for nirsevimab. It does not describe any programme. https://jpma.org.pk/index.php/public_html/article/view/20282
2–6. Not applicable. Nirsevimab may be available privately, but I could not confirm whether it is registered with DRAP (Pakistan's drug regulator).

### Flu
1. **Not routine.** Flu vaccine is not in the EPI schedule (same source as above). A published study also states that Pakistan's national programme does not include flu vaccine. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5893193
   - Some private hospitals give flu vaccine from 6 months. For example, the Aga Khan University Hospital children's vaccine list (Sept 2020) shows an annual flu shot from 6 months. This is hospital practice, not national policy: https://hospitals.aku.edu/pakistan/Health-Services/pharmacy/Documents/Children%20Vaccination%20List_Sept%202020_v3.pdf
   - I could not find a Pakistan Paediatric Association schedule.
2–4. Not applicable for "routine for all". If the app ever shows flu for PK as optional, northern-hemisphere timing (Sep–Nov) and the WHO/US 2-dose rule for first-timers under 9 would apply.

**Verdict for PK:** no RSV reminder and no flu reminder. The owner's belief is confirmed.

## United Kingdom (GB)

### RSV
1. **No infant antibody for all babies.** The UK protects babies through a maternal vaccine programme instead.
   - **Maternal Abrysvo:** offered from 28 weeks of pregnancy, all year round (not only in season), in every pregnancy. It can be given up to labour, but ideally at 28 weeks or soon after.
     - NHS page (reviewed 1 Sep 2024): https://www.nhs.uk/vaccinations/rsv-vaccine/
     - UKHSA guidance for health professionals (updated 11 Mar 2026): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/respiratory-syncytial-virus-rsv-programme-information-for-healthcare-professionals/rsv-vaccination-of-pregnant-women-for-infant-protection-information-for-healthcare-practitioners
   - **Infant nirsevimab (or clesrovimab) is only for:**
     - babies born before 32 weeks, who are too early to benefit from the maternal vaccine. All four UK nations have offered this since autumn 2025, in or just before the baby's first RSV season.
     - high-risk babies and young children.
     - Sources: NHS England, Jul 2025: https://www.england.nhs.uk/2025/07/nhs-to-roll-out-long-lasting-suit-of-armour-jab-to-protect-thousands-of-premature-babies-from-rsv/ ; Green Book RSV chapter, 3 Jun 2026: https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a2015a4730aece621f09866/Green_Book_RSV_20260603.pdf
   - The Green Book records that the JCVI (the UK's vaccine advisory committee) judges nirsevimab and clesrovimab suitable for a universal infant programme (clesrovimab advice from Oct 2025). However, the UK has chosen the maternal programme.
2. Season: about Oct–Mar, but this is not relevant to healthy babies.
3–4. Not applicable for healthy term babies.
5. High-risk and second-season doses: for specialists only.
6. Maternal vaccine: this is the UK's main strategy. A UKHSA report of 13 Aug 2026 says RSV intensive-care admissions of infants roughly halved, and that 61.7% of women giving birth had been vaccinated: https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/maternal-rsv-vaccine-halves-newborn-icu-admissions-says-ukhsa

**App implication:** no infant RSV reminder for GB. A pregnancy-stage reminder ("RSV vaccine from 28 weeks") would fit if Palna tracks pregnancy.

### Flu
1. **Not for healthy babies under 2.** The UK programme is age-based from 2 years. Source: National flu immunisation programme 2026 to 2027 letter (dated 26 Feb 2026, page updated 9 Jun 2026), which says the eligible groups are unchanged from last year: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-flu-immunisation-programme-plan-2026-to-2027/national-flu-immunisation-programme-2026-to-2027-letter
   - **All children aged 2 or 3 on 31 Aug 2026** get it at the GP. A child who turns 2 on or after 1 Sep is not included that season unless in a risk group.
   - **School-age children** from Reception to Year 11 get it at school.
   - **Children aged 6 months to under 18 in clinical risk groups** are also eligible.
2. Months: the children's programme starts **1 Sep 2026** (or as soon as vaccine arrives), and GPs are asked to prioritise 2–3 year olds. All schools should have a first visit by the end of Nov 2026, with second visits done by 11 Dec 2026.
3. Two doses:
   - **Healthy children get 1 dose of LAIV, even the first time.**
   - Two doses at least 4 weeks apart apply only to children **under 9 in a clinical risk group** who have never had a flu vaccine. This includes risk-group babies aged 6 months to under 2. Source: UKHSA poster "Which flu vaccine should children have? 2026 to 2027": https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a58d96c908b618a53702f09/UKHSA_13610_Children_Flu_Vaccine_A3_poster_update_2026-2027_WEB.pdf
4. Products:
   - Under 2 (risk groups only): injected cell-based vaccine (IIVc), or the egg-based one (IIVe) if IIVc is not available.
   - From 2 years: LAIV nasal spray. An injection (IIVc) is used if the spray is unsuitable, or if parents decline it because of its porcine gelatine. This option may matter to Muslim families.
   - All 2026–27 vaccines are trivalent.

**App implication for GB:**
- Flu reminder each September for children aged 2 or 3 on 31 August, then through school.
- No flu reminder at 6 months.
- No 2-dose message for healthy children.
- Suggested halal note: "Nasal spray contains pork gelatine; an injection can be requested."

## United States (US)

> **Policy is unstable in 2026.** Read this before the details.
> - On 5 Jan 2026 the CDC Acting Director issued a new childhood schedule. It moved flu to "shared clinical decision-making" and moved infant RSV antibody to the "high-risk groups" category.
> - On 16 Mar 2026 a federal court (AAP v. Kennedy, D. Mass.) stayed that schedule. The stay also covered 13 ACIP appointments and every ACIP vote after 11 Jun 2025. The CDC schedules mostly reverted to the 2025 versions.
> - The government appealed to the First Circuit, which heard argument on 6 Oct 2026. I found no decision yet.
> - An executive order of 10 Aug 2026 ("Delivering Gold Standard Childhood Vaccine Recommendations") again places flu under shared decision-making and RSV antibodies under high-risk groups. HHS was given 90 days to produce plans.
> - The AAP (American Academy of Pediatrics) still recommends flu vaccine for everyone from 6 months, and RSV protection for all infants.
>
> Sources:
> - Congressional Research Service (CRS) IN12684 (version of 29 Jul 2026): https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-07-29_IN12684_b816dec7ff4296abcd33d3866995ff17f80fd08e.html
> - CRS R48982 (11 Jun 2026): https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48982
> - Court case record: https://clearinghouse.net/case/46764/
> - Report of the 6 Oct appeal argument: https://www.medicaldaily.com/aap-kennedy-vaccine-lawsuit-paused-october-6-appeal-478666
> - Executive order coverage, Drug Topics, 10 Aug 2026: https://www.drugtopics.com/view/trump-signs-order-narrowing-childhood-vaccine-recommendations
> - AAP 2026–27 flu recommendations: https://www.healthychildren.org/English/news/Pages/aap-(influenza)-flu-prevention-recommendations-for-2026-27.aspx
>
> **Recommendation for the app:** keep US reminders, since the CDC page in effect and the AAP both support them. Keep the "ask your doctor" wording, and re-check after the First Circuit rules.

### RSV
1. **Yes, effectively universal.** CDC recommends one dose of nirsevimab or clesrovimab for **all infants under 8 months** born during or entering their first RSV season, when:
   - the mother did not get RSV vaccine in pregnancy, or
   - her vaccination status is unknown, or
   - the baby was born less than 14 days after her dose.

   There is no preference between the two products. Eligibility is based on the baby's age on the day the dose is given.
   - Source: CDC "RSV Immunization Guidance for Infants and Young Children" (last updated 18 Aug 2025): https://www.cdc.gov/rsv/hcp/vaccine-clinical-guidance/infants-young-children.html
   - The CDC schedule now displayed is the 2025 version (updated 2 Jul 2025), which shows nirsevimab "1 dose depending on maternal RSV vaccination status": https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-age.html
   - **Doubt about clesrovimab:** ACIP's June 2025 clesrovimab vote falls inside the court stay. CRS says it is "unclear whether a CDC/ACIP recommendation for clesrovimab remains", while the nirsevimab recommendation "remains in effect". The CDC clinical page still lists both.
2. Season: give doses **October through the end of March** in most of the continental US. Timing differs in Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico, southern Florida and some territories, so follow local guidance there.
   - Example state alert: Arkansas Department of Health, 18 Mar 2026: https://healthy.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/ADH-HAN-RSV-letterhead-3.18.2026.pdf. It says babies born May–Sep 2026 can get the dose from 1 Oct 2026.
3. Babies born 1 Oct–31 Mar: ideally during the birth hospital stay, or **within the first week after birth**.
4. Babies born Apr–Sep: shortly before the season, **ideally in October (or November)**, as long as the baby is still under 8 months at the time of the dose. Babies who missed it can get it "at the earliest opportunity" during the season, including at well-child visits.
5. Second season: nirsevimab only, for high-risk children aged 8–19 months (for example chronic lung disease of prematurity, severe immunocompromise, some cystic fibrosis, American Indian/Alaska Native children). Healthy babies aged 8 months or more should not get it.
6. Maternal Abrysvo (32–36 weeks, given September–January): the infant antibody is **not needed** if the baby was born **14 or more days** after the mother's dose. Rare exceptions, decided by the clinician, include a mother with immunocompromise or HIV. The owner's app note ("If the mother had the RSV vaccine in pregnancy, the baby may not need it") is correct.

### Flu
1. **Yes, under the CDC schedule now in effect:** annual flu vaccine for **everyone 6 months and older**.
   - The June 2025 ACIP reaffirmation of this falls inside the court stay. However, the restored 2025 schedule itself recommends it.
   - Under the stayed January 2026 schedule and the August 2026 executive order, it would become "shared clinical decision-making".
   - The AAP 2026–27 guidance says all children from 6 months should be vaccinated.
   - Source: CDC schedule (2025 version) above, which shows "1 or 2 doses annually" for young children: https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-age.html
2. Ages: from 6 months, with no upper limit. Months: ideally **September–October**, by the end of October. Children who need 2 doses should get the first dose as soon as vaccine is available, even in July or August.
3. Two doses at least 4 weeks apart: for children **aged 6 months through 8 years** (that is, under 9) who have **not had at least 2 doses of flu vaccine before 1 July** of the current season, or whose history is unknown. The two earlier doses do not need to be in the same season. This is wider than "first-time" only.
   - Example state guidance for 2026–27, NYC Department of Health: https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/letters/2026/2026-2027-seasonal-flu-vax-kick-off-vfc-flu-vaccine-ordering.pdf
4. Products: under 2 years, injection only. The nasal spray (FluMist) is licensed from 2 years. All 2026–27 US vaccines are trivalent. Thimerosal-free vaccines are the ones used, following a 2025 vote, although that vote is technically within the stay.

## Canada (CA)

### RSV
1. **NACI (Canada's national vaccine advisory committee) strongly recommends universal seasonal infant RSV programmes, but funding is decided by each province.**
   - Summary of the NACI statement of 10 Apr 2026: https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/publications/vaccines-immunization/national-advisory-committee-immunization-summary-updated-guidance-infants-children-respiratory-syncytial-virus-use-monoclonal-antibodies-nirsevimab-clesrovimab-rsvpref-vaccine.html
   - NACI allows two programme designs:
     - nirsevimab or clesrovimab for **all infants** in their first season, or
     - maternal RSVpreF plus antibody for babies whose mothers were not vaccinated and for high-risk infants.
   - Provinces in 2026–27, as far as I could verify:
     - **Ontario: universal.** Babies born on or after 1 Apr 2026 who are under 8 months when immunised. Doses from **1 Oct 2026**, finishing by 31 Mar. Page updated 15 Oct 2026: https://www.ontario.ca/page/respiratory-syncytial-virus-rsv-prevention-programs
     - **Quebec: universal.** Babies born on or after 1 April of the current year.
     - **Manitoba: universal from 2026.** Provincial news release, May 2026: https://news.gov.mb.ca/news/index.html?item=73599&posted=2026-05-01
     - **New Brunswick: universal.** Babies born from 1 Apr 2026 who are under 8 months. Given from mid-October to the end of March.
     - **Nova Scotia: universal.** Season defined as **25 Oct 2026 – 8 May 2027**. Covers babies born on or after 1 May 2026 who are under 8 months. Source: https://novascotia.ca/dhw/CDPC/documents/rsv-infant-immunization-information-healthcare-professionals.pdf
     - **Saskatchewan, PEI, Newfoundland and Labrador, Yukon, NWT, Nunavut: reported as universal.** Source: rsvprotect.ca, an advocacy site, not official; it warns it may be out of date: https://rsvprotect.ca/provincial-coverage-and-availability/
     - **Alberta: partial.** Free for babies **born in hospital during the season (November–April)**, plus the existing high-risk programme. There appears to be **no catch-up** for babies born before the season. Source: https://www.alberta.ca/respiratory-virus-season-get-vaccinated
     - **British Columbia: high-risk only.** This covers about 10% of births (premature babies, certain medical conditions, remote and Indigenous communities). The 2026–27 programme started 1 Sep 2026. Others can buy it privately. Source: Fraser Health, 16 Sep 2025: https://www.fraserhealth.ca/employees/medical-health-officer-updates/Respiratory-Syncytial-Virus-Immunoprophylaxis-Program-2025-2026-What-health-care-providers ; and BCCDC materials. **I did not find the official 2026–27 BC eligibility brochure.**
2. Season: usually about **November to April**. Provinces give doses from about **1 October** (Ontario, New Brunswick) or define their own dates (Nova Scotia 25 Oct–8 May; Alberta births November–April).
3. Born during the season: shortly after birth, before leaving hospital (Manitoba, Alberta, Ontario).
4. Born before the season: catch-up if **under 8 months on the day of the dose**. In practice this means babies born from 1 April (Ontario, Quebec, New Brunswick) or 1 May (Nova Scotia, Newfoundland and Labrador). Not in BC (unless high-risk) or Alberta.
5. Second season: high-risk children under 24 months get nirsevimab only. Clesrovimab is not authorised for the second season.
6. Maternal RSVpreF: NACI says 28–36 weeks; Health Canada's authorisation is 32–36 weeks. If the baby is born **less than 14 days** after the dose, the baby should get the antibody. Otherwise both together are "generally not recommended", except for high-risk babies such as those born before 37 weeks in Ontario. For later pregnancies, the antibody should be considered for the new baby, because there is no guidance on repeating the maternal vaccine.

**App implication:** treat Canada as "offered to all babies" with province-level caveats. If province is known, consider excluding BC (and Alberta catch-up). If not, keep "ask your doctor".

### Flu
1. **Yes.** NACI recommends flu vaccine every year for **everyone 6 months and older**, with emphasis on children under 5.
   - NACI statement 2025–2026 (30 Apr 2025): https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/publications/vaccines-immunization/national-advisory-committee-immunization-statement-seasonal-influenza-vaccines-2025-2026.html
   - The Canadian Paediatric Society says the 2026–27 NACI advice is "not substantially changed", and all vaccines are trivalent: https://cps.ca/en/documents/position/influenza-vaccine-children-youth
   - Provinces fund it for all residents aged 6 months and over. **I did not check each province's flu page individually.**
2. Ages: from 6 months. Months: as soon as vaccine arrives in the fall, usually **October** (Ontario starts at 1 Oct for most people). Flu can start circulating in October.
3. Two doses: children **aged 6 months to under 9** who have **never** had a flu vaccine get **2 doses at least 4 weeks apart**. Once they have had any flu vaccine, they get 1 dose per year. This differs slightly from the US rule: in Canada, one earlier dose is enough.
4. Products: ages 6–23 months get an injection only (standard-dose or cell-based Flucelvax; Influvac is not used under 3). The nasal spray (FluMist) is from 2 years and must not be used under 24 months.

## United Arab Emirates (AE)

### RSV
1. **Yes, for babies born during the season. This is national since 2025.**
   - **National:** Ministry of Health and Prevention (MOHAP) Ministerial Decree No. 189 of 2025 added RSV long-acting antibodies to the national immunisation programme. I could only read this as cited in the Sanofi summary below; I did not find the decree text.
   - **Abu Dhabi:** Department of Health (DoH) Circular No. 37/2026 (3 Mar 2026) adds the antibody (nirsevimab or clesrovimab) to the childhood programme from the 2025–26 season. It says a single dose "shall be administered to **all newborns born during the RSV season**", **at birth, together with Hepatitis B and BCG**. https://www.doh.gov.ae/-/media/Feature/Resources/Circulars/2026/37--2026.ashx (I read this via search excerpts; the PDF did not download.)
   - **Dubai:** Dubai Health Authority (DHA) Circular CIR-2026-00000027. A single dose **at birth** for babies born in season, "unless the mother received the RSV vaccine during that pregnancy".
   - Secondary source (Sanofi, written for health professionals; it is the manufacturer): https://pro.campus.sanofi/ae/respiratory-syncytial-virus/articles/rsv-prevention-guidelines-in-uae
2. Season: **1 September – 1 March**, the same in Abu Dhabi and Dubai. This is earlier than the owner's assumed Oct–Mar.
3. Born during the season: **at birth**, before leaving hospital, with Hepatitis B and BCG.
4. Born before the season: **I found no catch-up programme for babies born before 1 September.** The documents I found only cover babies born during the season. This differs from the US, Saudi Arabia and Canada.
5. Second season: nirsevimab only, for high-risk children aged 8–19 months.
6. Maternal vaccine:
   - Abu Dhabi guideline (Jan 2026, as summarised by Sanofi): the antibody is given if the mother was not vaccinated, if her status is unknown, or if the baby was born less than 14 days after her dose.
   - Dubai: no antibody if the mother was vaccinated during that pregnancy.
   - The circular itself says "all newborns". The wording conflicts, so keep "may not need it".

**App implication for AE:**
- Babies born 1 Sep – end of Feb: reminder at birth.
- No "before the season" catch-up reminder, or only a soft "ask your doctor whether your baby needs it" in late August. A pediatrician should decide which.
- Programmes differ by emirate (Abu Dhabi, Dubai, and MOHAP/EHS for the northern emirates).

### Flu
1. **Yes, recommended for everyone from 6 months.**
   - MOHAP's 2026–27 national campaign runs **1 Sep 2026 – 31 Mar 2027**. Flu vaccine "is available to everyone in the UAE from six months of age". Children aged 6 months and over are a priority group, and some sources say children aged 6 months–5 years. Source: Gulf News reporting MOHAP: https://gulfnews.com/uae/health/uae-flu-season-2026-how-to-protect-your-family-1.500673725 and https://gulfnews.com/uae/health/uae-flu-season-september-vaccine-doctors-warning-1.500677008
   - Cost:
     - Free for citizens and residents in high-risk groups.
     - Abu Dhabi (SEHA, the public health provider): reported free for children under 5 and all Thiqa card holders (Thiqa is the insurance for Emirati nationals).
     - Dubai: the DHA charges about AED 50.
   - Students are being added to the priority groups: https://www.khaleejtimes.com/uae-adds-students-hajj-umrah-travellers-to-priority-groups-for-flu-vaccination
   - **I could not find an official MOHAP or DoH page in English. All of this comes from UAE newspapers quoting MOHAP.**
2. Ages: from 6 months, with children under 5 a priority. Months: from **September**; the campaign runs September–March.
3. Two doses: **not confirmed from an official UAE source.** UAE practice follows WHO/CDC: children 6 months to under 9 having their first flu vaccine get 2 doses at least 4 weeks apart.
4. Products: injection for under 2s. A nasal spray is not commonly offered in the UAE; not verified.

## Saudi Arabia (SA)

### RSV
1. **Yes. Nirsevimab is in the national immunisation schedule.**
   - The Public Health Authority (Weqaya) updated the national schedule. The update gives "a single dose of nirsevimab… to **all infants born during the RSV season**", plus catch-up for **infants under 8 months entering their first season**. Babies are excluded if their mother had the RSV vaccine in pregnancy at least 14 days before birth.
   - Source: Sanofi summary of Weqaya recommendations, Jul 2026, reference dated Oct 2026: https://pro.campus.sanofi/sa/respiratory-syncytial-virus/articles/rsv-prevention-guidelines-in-ksa
   - Al Yaum newspaper (Arabic, 2026) reports that the newborn RSV antibody was added to the national schedule in **2025**, and that a **maternal RSV vaccine** is the newest addition for 2026: https://www.alyaum.com/articles/6677905/
   - The Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA) approved nirsevimab in Feb 2024 (Saudi bronchiolitis consensus, 2024): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11321529/
   - **I could not reach an official MOH or Weqaya page.** The MOH schedule card online is the Dec 2019 version and has no RSV entry: https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/HealthAwareness/EducationalContent/HealthTips/Documents/Immunization-Schedule.pdf
2. Season: **October – March**. Doses start **about 2 weeks before the season (mid-September)** and continue until it ends.
3. Born during the season: one dose shortly after birth.
4. Born before the season: **under 8 months when the season starts** get one dose at the start of the campaign (mid-September to October).
5. Second season: high-risk children aged 8–19 months (for example congenital heart disease).
6. Maternal vaccine: no antibody needed if the mother was vaccinated **at least 14 days** before birth. The maternal vaccine has reportedly been added to the national schedule in 2026; not confirmed officially.

**App implication for SA:** the owner's rule fits.
- Born mid-September to March: reminder at birth.
- Born before the season and under 8 months on 1 October: reminder in **mid-September**, which is earlier than the US October date.

### Flu
1. **Yes, from 6 months.**
   - The official MOH National Immunization Schedule card (Dec 2019 print, still the one linked on the MOH page, which shows "last modified 26/10/2026") lists **"Influenza" starting at 6 months**, with a yearly flu record row. Source: https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/HealthAwareness/EducationalContent/HealthTips/Documents/Immunization-Schedule.pdf
   - MOH campaigns recommend flu vaccine for everyone, name **children aged 6 months – 5 years** as a priority group, and give it free at health centres.
   - 2026 campaign: launched about 30 Sep 2026, lasting 4 months, with booking via the Sehhaty app. Source (Najran region): https://www.moh.gov.sa/en/Ministry/MediaCenter/News/Pages/news-2026-09-30-002.aspx
2. Ages: from 6 months (priority 6 months–5 years). Months: **late September/October to about January**.
3. Two doses: **not found on an official MOH page.** KAUST Health, which cites the MOH flu FAQ, gives 2 doses at least 28 days apart for children aged 6 months–8 years having their first vaccine, or who have had only 1 dose before. Source: https://kh.kaust.edu.sa/pages-org/news/detail/2021/11/02/get-protected-this-flu-season
4. Products: injection for under 2s.

## Main doubts / to verify with the pediatrician
1. **US:** recommendations are in legal limbo (court stay vs. Aug 2026 executive order). If the First Circuit lifts the stay, flu becomes "shared decision-making" and RSV becomes "high-risk group" in the CDC schedule. Clesrovimab's CDC status is formally unclear.
2. **UAE catch-up:** no programme found for babies born before 1 September. Also, "all newborns" (Abu Dhabi circular) conflicts with the maternal-vaccine exception (Abu Dhabi guideline, DHA).
3. **UAE/Saudi season dates differ from Oct–Mar:**
   - UAE: born 1 Sep – 1 Mar.
   - Saudi: Oct–Mar season, but doses start mid-September.
   - Reminders should use per-country months, not one northern-hemisphere rule.
4. **Saudi RSV:** I could not reach an official MOH or Weqaya document. The details come from Sanofi's summary of Weqaya recommendations and Saudi press.
5. **Canada:** universal in most provinces, but BC is high-risk only and Alberta covers only in-hospital births November–April. Province-level data partly comes from rsvprotect.ca (not official).
