## A letter to the reviewing doctor

Dear Doctor,

Thank you for agreeing to review Palna. Palna is a free baby-tracking app for families. Parents use it to record feeds, sleep, diapers, growth, vaccinations, medicines, symptoms, first foods and milestones, and to share the record with the rest of the family. It is used in many countries and is available in English, Urdu and Roman Urdu.

Alongside the records, Palna shows some medical information: national vaccine schedules for 23 countries, optional RSV and flu reminders, growth checks against the WHO growth standards, warning signs, emergency numbers, a starting-solids guide with recipes, and developmental milestones. All of it was taken from official sources (national immunisation programmes, WHO, NHS, CDC), but none of it has yet been checked by a clinician. That is what we are asking you to do.

What we ask. Please read each item in this pack and tell us whether it is correct, and what to change if it is not. Where our sources disagree or we had to make a choice, we list it as an open question at the end of the section.

How to mark. Every table has a “Reviewer ✓ / ✗ / ?” column and a “Change to / comment” column. Write ✓ if the item is correct; ✗ if it is wrong, with the correction in the next column; ? if you are unsure or want to comment. If a whole table is correct, one ✓ next to its heading is enough. For open questions, tick the box and write your answer on the line. You can mark on paper or in Word (tracked changes are welcome). It is fine to skip countries outside your experience: just mark them ?.

Where to start. If time is short, please start with “Questions we most need answered” on the next page. They are the items where we are least sure.

Important. Palna is not a medical device. It does not diagnose and does not calculate medicine doses; the app, its store listing and its terms of use tell parents to follow their own doctor’s advice. We want the information it does show to be correct and safely worded.

How long. About 3 to 4 hours in total: vaccine schedules 90–120 minutes (most items are routine), RSV and flu 15 minutes, wording parents see 30 minutes, emergency numbers 10 minutes, solids guide 30 minutes, milestones 15 minutes. Please review the English only; the Urdu and Roman Urdu translations follow the English and will be checked separately once your corrections are in.

Questions about this pack: hello@palnacare.com. Thank you for your time and care.

With thanks,

The Palna team, Toolbox Services (SMC-Private) Limited

## 1. Vaccine schedules (23 countries)

For each country the app shows the national routine schedule for all babies, from birth to about 6 years. Each dose is shown with its due date (birth date plus the due age), and parents get a reminder the day before, on the day, and when it is overdue. Above the list the app says: “Always confirm dates with your doctor or nurse; they can differ by region and vaccine brand” (wording in section 3.9).

Rules we followed for every country: where the source gives an age range, the earliest recommended age is used; seasonal vaccines (flu, COVID), RSV protection, risk-group-only, regional and travel vaccines are left out (RSV and flu are optional reminders, section 2); school-age doses after about 6 years are left out. Parents can add any other vaccine by name.

“Verified” shows whether a clinician has checked the item. Nothing is verified yet: after your review we will mark each confirmed item as verified. “—” in the Dose column means the app shows no dose number (a single dose).

In total: 405 doses in 23 countries. Countries in this section: 1.1 Pakistan, 1.2 United Kingdom, 1.3 United States, 1.4 Canada, 1.5 United Arab Emirates, 1.6 Saudi Arabia, 1.7 India, 1.8 Bangladesh, 1.9 Qatar, 1.10 Kuwait, 1.11 Oman, 1.12 Bahrain, 1.13 Malaysia, 1.14 Australia, 1.15 Germany, 1.16 Ireland, 1.17 Italy, 1.18 Spain, 1.19 France, 1.20 Netherlands, 1.21 Norway, 1.22 Sweden, 1.23 Türkiye.

## 2. Optional RSV and flu reminders

Next to the routine schedule, the app shows RSV protection and the flu vaccine as “Optional – ask your doctor”, but only in countries that offer them to all babies or young children (not only to risk groups). Families can hide these reminders.

How the RSV reminder works. A baby born inside the country’s window gets a reminder due on the day of birth. A baby born before the window gets a catch-up reminder on the catch-up date, but only if still under the age limit on that date. The reminder disappears at the end of the window if no dose is recorded. One RSV reminder per baby (first season only).

How the flu reminder works. One reminder per season. It is due on the season start date, or on the day the child reaches the minimum age if that is later, and is shown for 6 months. If no flu vaccine has been recorded before and the child is under the two-dose age, the app adds the two-dose note. In the United Kingdom and Ireland it adds the nasal-spray note.
